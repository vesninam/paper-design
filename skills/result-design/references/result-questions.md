# Questions for designing a scientific result

Use this reference to develop a planned result through a focused conversation. Ask only questions whose answers affect the design. Reuse existing decisions and record missing information as open rather than manufacturing precision. These are design prompts, not a mandatory experiment recipe.

## A. Content — What does the result present?

1. **(What?) What exactly is presented, and in what form?**

   Describe the content and format of the result. For example, a table of solar flare parameters, a heatmap showing relationships between input variables, or a diagram illustrating the operating algorithm of a data collection system.

2. **(Why?) What scientific question does this result answer?**

   Describe what can be learned from the result without repeating the answer to Question 1. For example, a heatmap might indicate that the total amount of plasma in the ionosphere is primarily influenced by ultraviolet radiation.

3. **(Focus of attention) What will the reader understand from this result, and will it match what you intended to communicate? Could the same result be presented more effectively?**

   Use this question to assess whether the chosen presentation communicates the intended scientific message clearly.

## B. Motivation (Rationale) — Why is this result needed?

4. **(Subsequent use) What role does this result play in the paper?**

   Describe its role: providing motivation, demonstrating a phenomenon, validating a method or finding, etc.

5. **(Conclusions or comparisons) How does this result contribute to the conclusions or discussion of the paper?**

   For example, it may enable comparison with findings from other researchers or allow us to draw conclusion A rather than conclusion B.

6. **(Occam's razor) What would happen if we simply removed this result or part of it (e.g., a figure panel)?**

   A simple criterion: if nothing changes because the same information can be obtained elsewhere, consider removing the result as redundant or moving it to the appendix.

## C. What makes the result sufficient?

7. **(Sufficiency) Which cases, parameters, and metrics must be presented?**

8. **(Underlying assumptions) What limitations or assumptions are involved in obtaining this result? Could they introduce an artifact or obscure a real effect?**

9. **(Possible outcomes) What outcomes do we anticipate, and how would each affect the paper?**

   Since the result has not yet been obtained from actual data, several outcomes are possible. For example, a correlation may or may not be present. Consider what each outcome would mean for the paper.

10. **(Experimental design) Does the proposed experiment genuinely address the scientific question being investigated?**

## D. How does this result connect to the rest of the paper?

11. **(Interconnections) Does this result depend on material presented earlier in the paper, including other results? Which other parts of the paper are connected to this result?**

12. **(Additional data) Is any further data processing needed to provide additional confirmation of this result?**
