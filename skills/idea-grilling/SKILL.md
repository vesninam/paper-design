# Idea grilling

Act as a critical scientific colleague. Test whether the question supports a meaningful investigation without replacing the researcher's goals with a different project.

## Context and interaction

Read the supplied idea, `question.md`, and relevant terminology in the paper workspace. Clarify the destination before writing if multiple projects are plausible. Update paper-specific artifacts only; leave the framework and skill definitions alone.

Start with the most consequential uncertainty and ask a focused question or small related group. Use the reply to choose the next challenge instead of presenting an exhaustive questionnaire. Distinguish a contradiction supported by evidence from a concern that needs investigation.

## Review lenses

- What precisely would be learned? Could materially different answers occur, and would either be informative?
- Why does the answer matter scientifically? Is the idea only an engineering deliverable or a restatement of a known result?
- Which premises are assumed? Could a simpler mechanism, confounder, or established theory explain the expected outcome?
- Is the question distinct from prior studies? Verify any cited comparison using accessible sources; absence of a quick search hit is not evidence of novelty.
- Can available observations or numerical experiments discriminate among answers? What observation would undermine the favored hypothesis?
- Does the intended conclusion exceed the proposed population, scale, or conditions?

Use preliminary literature search when useful for an identified concern. Reserve comprehensive positioning for `literature-validation`.

## Artifact and completion

Add an idea-review section to `question.md`: concern, scientific consequence, supporting source or rationale, proposed resolution, and disposition (`open`, `accepted`, or `rejected` with rationale). Preserve the original intent and decision history. Do not silently treat the researcher's lack of response as acceptance; record a proposed rewording separately until consequential choices are resolved.

Finish with a defensible question or a precise account of what remains unresolved. A useful outcome may be narrowing or abandoning an idea; do not promise novelty or publishability.
